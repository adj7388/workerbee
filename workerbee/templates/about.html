What's Spelling Bee?

If you're not familiar with Spelling Bee, take a look at the Spelling Bee site. Long story short: it's a word game published by the New York Times in which you create words from a set of letters. A new puzzle with a new set of letters appears every day, and most people try to reach "Genius" level by, well, finding a lot of words.

Quick aside: reaching "Genius" level unfortunately does not mean you're a real genius.

Are you affiliated with the New York Times?

No. I'm just a subscriber and a Spelling Bee solver.

Then why did you create this app?

Through years of playing Spelling Bee, my goal every day was to reach the Genius level, which most people count as having solved that day's puzzle.

But even if you reach Genius level there are still more words left to find —and if you find all the rest, you are crowned Queen Bee for the day!

I was always curious about which words I had missed or overlooked on the way to Genius. Beyond curiosity, knowing which words you have missed—especially if they are words you don't even know—is useful in solving future puzzles.

Couldn't you just wait and look at the solution the next day?

Well, yes, I suppose so. But that's no fun! Beyond that, though, wondering about the words I'd missed also started me thinking about the software that was used to create the puzzle in the first place. They clearly weren't doing it manually. They had to have some way to find all the words for any random set of letters, then a process for choosing which words to include in the puzzle and which to leave out.

I had some ideas about how one might do that so, just for fun, I decided to give it a shot.

So besides cheating at Spelling Bee 😄 what else can you do with the app?

Cheating?! Why, I've never been so insulted! 😄 (Use it to cheat if you want to. I won't judge 😄.) Seriously though, using software to create words from a random set of letters also got me interested in English word lists.

Before writing a line of code, I had a problem to solve: where could I get a good English language dictionary. Not exactly a dictionary. I didn't need defiitions. What I really needed was just a list of English words. Definitions would just require more storage and clutter things up. I could always link to definitions later, which this app does.

But finding an open source word list was harder than I expected.

First Stab

I eventually found a list hiding in a dusty corner of the internet. It contained more than 370,000 English words, and no definitions. Initially, I thought it would be just what I needed. It wasn't.

That uncurated, random list with a bazillion words was full of abbreviations, misspellings, proper nouns, esoteric words and all manner of cruft that were of no use as Spelling Bee words. That list worked fine as a way to get started, but once I had an initial working version of the app, I really needed a better word list.

SCOWL to the Rescue

That was when I came upon the SCOWL site, where some very smart people have joined forces to create not just one good English language word list, but several. Primarily for spell-checking software, they have created lists in American, British, Canadian, and other flavors of English. This is what I was looking for. In fact, there are several lists just of American English.

But that created a different problem. It wasn't clear exactly which list would work best for what I was doing: finding Spelling Bee words from a given set of letters.

Curiosity struck again so, being a bit of a word nerd, I created the Show Summaries feature to allow me to compare the various SCOWL lists and to assess how each performed as a source for Spelling Bee words.

Show Summaries

Thus was born the Show Summaries feature. Find Words only searches one list at a time, but Show Summaries searches all of the supported word lists simultaneously and produces a table comparing them including, among other things, all the words from all the word lists together in one place.

Another quick aside: Show Summaries is good for comparing the lists, but Find Words may be better for working with a specific puzzle (i.e., finding the words for a given set of letters) since it displays words in a way that mimics how the Spelling Bee hints page and Spelling Bee Buddy display hints: i.e., grouped either by initial letters or by word length.

Does anyone but you care about this stuff?

Probably not. But, it's mildly interesting word esoterica. Somebody may care for some reason I'm not even aware of. Might as well put it out there on the interwebs.
