What's Spelling Bee?

If you're not familiar with Spelling Bee, take a look at the Spelling Bee site. Long story short: it's a word game published by the New York Times in which you create words from a set of letters. A new puzzle with a new set of letters appears every day, and most people try to reach "Genius" level by (again, long story short) finding a lot of words. (As an aside and unfortunately, reaching "Genius" level does not mean you're a real genius.)

Are you affiliated with the New York Times then?

Nope. I'm just one of their customers and a Spelling Bee solver.

Why did you create this app?

Through years of playing Spelling Bee, my goal every day was to reach GABM - Genius All By Myself - sort of "winning" without using any hints—a bit like solving a crossword puzzle without looking anything up.

But even when you reach Genius level there are still more words to find (if you find them all you are crowned Queen Bee!) and I always wondered what words I had missed on the way to "Genius". Did I miss them because I was playing poorly, or did I miss them because they words I simply didn't know? There was no way to know without an app like this one.

I was also curious about how to write software to create or analyze word games like Spelling Bee. I had worked on many software projects in the past, but this was something different. It was a new challenge.

Besides cheating at Spelling Bee 😄 what else can you do with the app?

TLDR; Well, it uses several SCOWL lists as word sources. So if you're a Spelling Bee solver who's looking to find and learn new words, you can play around with Show Summaries, which searches all of the supported word lists simultaneously and shows word counts, pangrams, and other stuff from each of them. It can optionally show all the words found in every word list, all together in one place.

Otherwise, Find Words may be more useful in looking for words for a particular puzzle since it displays words in a way that mimics both the Spelling Bee hints page and the way Spelling Bee Buddy (a hint tool that comes with the game) displays words you've found, and clues about ones you haven't found.

The too long part ... When I was planning out this project, I realized that I would need an excellent source of English words (I know, duh, right?). I finally found a random word list that contained more than 370,000 English words, including a lot of crufty abbreviations, foreign words, and what-not. It didn't work very well because it just had too many words that would never be part of a Spelling Bee game. The useful words were buried in a sea of noise.

Eventually, I stumbled onto the SCOWL site (OK, I must confess I didn't exactly stumble onto it. ChatGPT told me about it.) where some very smart people have joined forces to create not just one word list, but several. It's an impressive array of lists mainly, though not exclusively, for spell-checking applications in American, British, Canadian, and other flavors of English.

Explore the SCOWL site for an exhaustive explanation of all the word lists there, how they were compiled, what's in them, and all the details you could ever want about lists of English words. There is a lot to know and they know it all.
