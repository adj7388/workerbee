What's Spelling Bee?

If you're not familiar with Spelling Bee, take a look at the Spelling Bee site. Long story short: it's a word game published by the New York Times in which you create words from a set of letters. A new puzzle with a new set of letters appears every day, and most people try to reach "Genius" level by (again, long story short) finding a lot of words. (As an aside, reaching "Genius" level unfortunately does not mean you're a real genius.)

Are you affiliated with the New York Times?

No. I'm just a subscriber and a Spelling Bee solver.

Then why did you create this app?

Through years of playing Spelling Bee, my goal every day was to reach the Genius level, which is what passes for solving that day's Spelling Bee.

However, even if you reach Genius level there are still more words left to find —and if you find all the rest you are crowned Queen Bee or the day!

I was always curious about which words I had missed or overlooked on the way to Genius. Beyond curiosity, knowing which words you have missed—especially if they are words you don't even know—is useful in solving future puzzles.

Couldn't you just wait and look at the solution the next day?

Well, yes, I suppose so. But wondering about the words I'd missed also started me thinking about the software that was used to create the puzzle in the first place. Surely they had some way to find all the words for any random set of letters, then some process for choosing which words to include in the puzzle and which to leave out.

In any case, I assumed they must have software to manage that process, and I started to wonder how you would write a software tool to help with that. I had some ideas about how to do it so, just for fun, I decided to give it a shot.

So besides cheating at Spelling Bee 😄 what else can you do with the app?

Cheating?! Why, I've never been so insulted! 😄 Seriously though, beyond finding words from a random set of letters, doing this project also got me interested in English word lists.

When I set out to write the app, I remember thinking of the first problem I needed to solve: how to obtain a good English language dictionary. That wasn't entirely correct though. What I really needed was just a list of English words. Not only did I not need dictionary definitions, they would just clutter things up and require more storage. (I could always link to definitions later, which this app does.)

Finding an open source word list was harder than I expected.

First Stab

I eventually found a word list floating around the internet. It contained more than 370,000 English words, and no definitions. Perfect, I thought. Not so perfect, I soon learned.

Unfortunately, an uncurated, random list with so many words was almost guaranteed to be cluttered with cruft: abbreviations, misspellings, and esoteric words that would never appear in Spelling Bee. It worked fine as a way to get started, but it had way too many words, too much noise, and was just a drag on the final product.

SCOWL to the Rescue

Once I had a working version of the app, I really needed a better word list. That's when I stumbled onto the SCOWL site, where some very smart people have joined forces to create not just one good list of English words, but several well curated spell-checking lists in American, British, Canadian, and other flavors of English. This is what I was looking for.

But now there was a different problem. I now had not just one word list, but several from which to choose. And it wasn't clear exactly which one would work was best for Spelling Bee.

So, again out of curiosity (and being a bit of a word nerd), I added the Show Summaries feature to allow me to explore how each of those different SCOWL lists performed as a source for Spelling Bee words, and compare them to each other.

Show Summaries

Thus was born the Show Summaries feature. Find Words only searches one list at a time, but Show Summaries searches all of the supported word lists simultaneously and, among other things, can show all the words from every word list all together in one place.

Find Words is probably more useful for working with a specific puzzle, since it displays words in a way that mimics how the Spelling Bee hints page and Spelling Bee Buddy display hints, grouped either by initial letters or by word length.

Spelling Bee already has hints? So it's not cheating, it's just more hints!

Hey, do with it what you will. I won't judge. 😄
