What's Spelling Bee?

It's a word game published by the New York Times. If you're not familiar with it, take a look at the Spelling Bee site.

Are you affiliated with the New York Times then?

No. Not at all. Just a Spelling Bee solver.

Why did you create this app?

Out of curiosity. Through years of playing Spelling Bee, my goal every day was to reach GABM - Genius All By Myself. (GABM will be explained somewhere in that Spelling Bee link above).

But even when you reach Genius level, that's not the end. There are still more words and, if you find all the rest, you are crowned Queen Bee! So after reaching Genius level, I always wondered what words I had missed along the way.

I like word games, and I was curious about how to write software to create games like Spelling Bee. I had worked on plenty of software projects in the past, but never anything like Spelling Bee, so it was an interesting challenge.

Bottom line is I created it for fun. I could learn new words by discovering the ones I missed on my way to Genius, get better at solving Spelling Bee, and have fun fiddling around with an interesting software project.

Besides cheating at Spelling Bee 😄, is this app useful for anything else?

TLDR; Well, it uses several SCOWL lists as word sources. So if you're a Spelling Bee solver that's looking to find and learn new words, you can play around with Show Summaries, which searches all of the supported word lists simultaneously and shows word counts, pangrams, and other stuff from each of them.

Otherwise, Find Words has a more useful display when searching just one word list and finding potential Spelling Bee words.

Explore the SCOWL site for an exhaustive explanation of all the word lists there, how they were compiled, what's in them, and all the details you could ever want about lists of English words. There is a lot to know and they know it all.

More Details: When I was planning out this project, I realized that, to find Spelling Bee words from a set of random letters, you need an excellent source of English words (I know, duh). After much searching, I finally found a random list online that contained more than 370,000 English words, and lot of crufty abbreviations, foreign words and what-not. That list often produced hundreds of Spelling Bee words—way too many to be useful. The signal to noise ratio was extremely low.

Luckily, I stumbled onto the SCOWL site where some very smart people have joined forces to create not just one word list, but several. It's an impressive array of lists mainly, though not exclusively, for spell checking in American, British, Australian, and other flavors of English.

So if you are interested in words or exploring word lists, or you're having trouble finding the right list to use for a given Spelling Bee puzzle, you may want to play around with Show Summaries, which searches all the word lists simultaneously and displays a summary of what it finds in all of them, optionally including the actual words from each list.
